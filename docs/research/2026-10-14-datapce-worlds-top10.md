# datapce worlds programme: top-10 finish (2026-10-14)

Nine isolated research worlds ran cycle 1 on 2026-10-13: six AI-era worlds (distributed systems, ML, BI demand, BI landscape, market, an ordinary-effort control) and three category worlds the owner added (games, security, tools). Both runs stopped on the usage limit overnight; their saved results were condensed and the owner's top 10 were finished in one economy run (21 agents), with a light Perplexity-like answer-engine world alongside. Web search was exhausted for the whole run; every number comes from direct page reads and public APIs. Labels: MEASURED / PLAUSIBLE / ASSUMED as the agents reported them.

## Headline

Eleven ideas went through the red team and two survive, both small paid games: a coding-puzzle game and a free browser idle game later sold on Steam. Neither is a million-dollar business. A good solo result is roughly $70k-$185k over the game's life, and a typical result is about $5k-$10k. All the business and security tools are dead because someone already sells them or gives them away.

## Ranked, highest priority first

### 1. Coding-puzzle game: you solve each level by writing small programs (The Farmer Was Replaced style), $6.99-$9.99 on Steam — GO-IF-TEST-PASSES

- **Why:** People pay for this kind of game. Of 128 paid coding games released since 2024, about 1 in 4 reached 200 reviews. That is a much less crowded field than idle games, which now see 200+ new releases a month. The owner is a programmer, which is an edge here. The famous million-dollar example is not a fair yardstick: it spent three years in early access and had a co-publisher.
- **Biggest risk:** Nobody hears about it. The owner has no proven audience among programmers. Free rivals are popular (Upload Labs has 8,740 reviews, Bitburner about 6,000). The typical new game in this niche has only about 45 reviews, which is about $5k-$10k.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: three solo-credited games, Replicube (742 reviews, $9.99), Auto Rogue (729, $6.99) and Wireworks (641, $6.99). 33 of 128 paid coding games since 2024 reached 200 reviews; 7 reached 1,000; the median is about 45. The famous one, The Farmer Was Replaced, sold 500,000 copies at $9.99 over about 3 years. ASSUMED conversion: a Replicube-level result is about $70k-$185k over the game's life.
- **Unit economics:** About $5 net per copy at $9.99 (ASSUMED, after Steam's cut). $25k a year needs about 5,000 copies, roughly 100-170 new reviews a year. That is top-quartile but reachable. $100k a year needs about 20,000 copies a year, which is the ceiling of the best solo comparables. Build: 6-12 months to an early-access version (ASSUMED). Running cost is about $0, plus a one-time $100 Steam fee.
- **14-day test:** Days 1-7: build a free browser demo with a grid, a tiny Python-like language and 5 puzzles. Host it on the owner's own site and on itch.io, with an email sign-up box and, optionally, a Steam wishlist page ($100). Days 8-14: the owner posts once on Hacker News and in two relevant subreddits, and emails about 10 coding streamers. PASS: at least 500 Steam wishlists, or at least 150 email sign-ups from at least 1,500 players, and at least 25% of players who start puzzle 1 finish puzzle 5. KILL: under 150 wishlists and under 50 sign-ups, or under 10% finishing puzzle 5.

### 2. Free browser idle game first, then a $5-$12 paid version on Steam — GO-IF-TEST-PASSES

- **Why:** The red team said this never works for one person, but there is a counter-example: one solo developer (Orb of Creation) kept a free browser version and sells a $11.99 Steam version with 2,263 reviews. It is weaker than the coding game for two reasons. Idle games on Steam are flooding, from about 48 new releases a month in 2024 to 206-273 a month now. And the success story took years. My own check found Orb of Creation was already on Steam in early access in July 2022, so its 2,263 reviews built up over about four years, not since its June 2026 full release.
- **Biggest risk:** Speed and crowding. The comparables needed 4-5 years. Famous free web idles that sold paid versions themselves got under 100 reviews (Kittens Game 83, A Dark Room 74). Free web players often expect the game to stay free. There is no proof that the browser step beats an ordinary Steam demo.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: Orb of Creation, self-published at $11.99, 2,263 reviews, Steam early-access updates since 2022-07-15 (fetched today). Magic Research, $4.99, 419 reviews. About 9.8% of paid idle games reach 300 reviews (roughly $25k). 13.1% of 2026 one-person idle games at $5-$8 reach about $25k. ASSUMED: Orb of Creation is worth about $0.46M net over its life.
- **Unit economics:** About 55% of list price reaches the developer (PLAUSIBLE). $25k a year needs about 5,700 copies at $7.99, about 185 reviews. $100k a year needs about 22,800 copies, about 735 reviews. A small web prototype takes 2-4 weeks and the full game 3-6 months (ASSUMED). Hosting is under $200 a year.
- **14-day test:** Same shape as the coding game, so one demo can test both: a 30-60 minute free browser idle prototype on the owner's site and itch.io, with an email form and an anonymous return-visit counter, posted once on r/incremental_games. PASS: at least 1,500 plays, at least 20% of players returning on a later day, and at least 150 sign-ups or 200 wishlists. KILL: under 500 plays, under 10% returning, or under 50 sign-ups.

### 3. Small idle game that sits at the edge of your screen while you work, $4-$6 on Steam — NO-GO

- **Why:** People like these games, but about 266 came out between January 2025 and September 2026 and another 149 are listed for late 2026. The typical one has about 15 English reviews. The two biggest are free. A 'won't distract you' setting is easy for any rival to copy.
- **Biggest risk:** Saturation. Supply grows about 1.6-1.8x every half-year, and the free giants (Bongo Cat with 112,572 reviews, Task Bar Hero with 47,348) set the price at $0.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: in the 2025 group, 20 of 88 games (23%) cleared roughly the $25k line, and that includes free and publisher-backed titles. 68% have fewer than about 100 reviews. The breakout hit, Rusty's Retirement, has 14,906 reviews. A median game nets under $6k (ASSUMED arithmetic).
- **Unit economics:** About $2.05 net per copy at $4.19. $25k needs about 12,200 copies; $100k needs about 48,800. Build: 3-6 months (ASSUMED). About a 1-in-6 chance of about $25k for a solo 2027 launch.
- **14-day test:** Only if the owner overrides the no-go: a free itch.io prototype plus a landing page. Pass would need at least 1,000 plays, at least 300 sign-ups, and at least 30% saying they would pay $4.99. Even a pass would not overturn the crowding finding.

### 4. Text or menu-based idle online multiplayer game, paid for by supporter tiers (no ads, no pay-to-win) — NO-GO

- **Why:** The money is real. A one-employee Finnish studio behind Idle Clans booked about 284,000 euros in 2025, re-checked in the public registry. But that studio is a lone survivor. Since 2024, none of the new games of this kind that one person built from scratch reached 1,000 Steam reviews. Running a live online game alone also means servers, cheaters and ban disputes, indefinitely.
- **Biggest risk:** Long odds and a 1-2 year build before any answer. A 14-day test cannot measure what decides it: whether players are still paying after a year.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: Isam Games Oy (Idle Clans) had EUR 284k revenue in 2025, down 7.5%, with 1 employee. 4 of 54 games of this kind in the 2024-26 Steam group reached 1,000 reviews, and none was a solo, from-scratch menu game. 0 of 11 one-person phone versions from 2025-26 reached 500 ratings; the median is 16.
- **Unit economics:** $25k a year is about 417 supporters at $5 a month. At Melvor Idle's ratio of about 3.9% paying, that needs a community of about 11-12k. $100k a year needs about 1,670 supporters, a community of about 45k. Build: 12-24 months (ASSUMED). Servers cost about $20-200 a month (ASSUMED).
- **14-day test:** Only if reopened: a landing page plus a free single-player browser mock, posted once on r/incremental_games. Pass: at least 500 sign-ups and at least 60 non-binding '$5 a month' pledges. Kill: under 200 sign-ups or under 20 pledges.

### 5. Two copycat games: a Balatro-style luck roguelike ($5-$8), and a walking RPG where real-world steps drive the game — NO-GO

- **Why:** Luck roguelike: none of the 35 one-person games like this released in 2026 at $5-$8 reached even a modest sales level. Walking RPG: the model we were copying (WalkScape) takes two full-time people and is still unreleased after four years. The only lead worth a separate look is a simpler fantasy-themed walking tracker like Fantasy Hike.
- **Biggest risk:** Copying a breakout hit into a flood of copycats, and, for the walking game, a build far longer than one person's months.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: of 223 paid one-person roguelike-deckbuilders released in 2026, the median has 10 reviews. WalkScape takes $16,376.79 a month in supporter pledges. Fantasy Hike's maker (Forge7 AB, 3 employees) had SEK 5.47M turnover in 2025 and 14,994 iOS ratings. How much of that turnover comes from the game is ASSUMED.
- **Unit economics:** Roguelike: about 250-300 reviews needed to reach about $25k, and 0 of 35 recent solo entries in this price band got there. Walking RPG: about 420 supporters at $5 a month for $25k a year, after a multi-year build.
- **14-day test:** None recommended for either. If the owner is curious about the walking tracker, it needs its own fresh desk check first.

### 6. Citation checker for small law firms: checks that cited cases exist, that quotes match, and that the case supports the point — NO-GO

- **Why:** The problem is real and growing: courts have issued 2,184 decisions about AI-invented citations, about 3-4 new ones a day. But BriefCatch already sells exactly these three checks for $60 per lawyer a month, which is our own planned price. It also sells to courts.
- **Biggest risk:** The product already exists from an incumbent with better legal data at the same price.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: BriefCatch Individual plan $60 a seat per month with the citation checker included; Clearbrief $300 a month; median US lawyer penalty $2,500 across 172 cases; 2,184 court decisions on AI hallucinations.
- **Unit economics:** Would need to undercut $60 a seat. About 8-12 weeks to build (ASSUMED), plus ongoing accuracy work because mistakes carry legal liability.
- **14-day test:** None. The only thing that could revive it is hands-on proof that BriefCatch misses most of the bad citations courts identified.

### 7. Script-tamper checking for small online shops' payment pages (a card-industry security rule) — NO-GO

- **Why:** Since 2025 the card-industry rule no longer applies to most small shops, which hand card entry to Stripe, Shopify or similar. The shops still covered can get the check free (c/side up to 2,000 page views a month; Cloudflare on its free plan) or for about $99 a month.
- **Biggest risk:** A shrinking group of buyers, free competitors, and no relationships with the auditors or agencies who would bring in customers.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: the card-industry standards body (PCI SSC) dropped the rule for most simple shops in its post of 30 Jan 2025. c/side has a free tier and a $99-$499 a month paid tier. No public count of paying shops exists.
- **Unit economics:** $25k a year would need about 43 sites at $49 a month. Every customer can drop to a $0 tier. Build: 4-8 weeks (ASSUMED).
- **14-day test:** Only if overridden: a landing page aimed at agencies. Pass would need at least 15 business sign-ups from shops still covered by the rule, at least 5 of them with 5 or more stores.

### 8. Flaky-test quarantine: automatically sidelining unreliable software tests — NO-GO

- **Why:** A well-funded competitor gives the same feature away free to small teams (Trunk). Datadog sells the full workflow, and about a dozen free open-source tools exist.
- **Biggest risk:** Nothing left to charge for, and no measured dollar loss from the problem.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: BuildPulse charges $99, $249 and $499 a month; Datadog $20 per developer a month; Trunk is free for up to 5 developers. No measured cost per incident.
- **Unit economics:** Would have to undercut free. No realistic path to $25k a year for one person.
- **14-day test:** None.

### 9. Suggested spending limits for company AI usage, based on its own past spending — NO-GO

- **Why:** The big surprise bills (about $38k, about EUR 54k, about $30k) happened because limits did not switch off in time, not because people did not know what limit to set. Amazon already offers budgets that set themselves from past spending.
- **Biggest risk:** The real problem is the cloud provider's enforcement, which a third party cannot fix.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: the bills above, none of which blames the limit level. The top public feature request about spending limits has 21 reactions, below the 50 the charter requires.
- **Unit economics:** No paying-buyer signal on any route that could be measured.
- **14-day test:** None.

### 10. Cutting monitoring bills by suggesting which monitoring data to keep or drop (Datadog, Honeycomb) — NO-GO

- **Why:** Datadog and Honeycomb already do it themselves. Funded startups sell it too: Sawmills from $2,500 a month, and Grepr with a permanent free tier.
- **Biggest risk:** Built into the platforms and given away free by funded rivals.
- **Measured money:** MEASURED: Sawmills raised $10M and charges from $2,500 a month; Grepr is free, then $0.20 per GB; Datadog's native feature is already included in its price.
- **Unit economics:** No price room above free and native features. Customers would also demand security certifications that one person cannot easily get.
- **14-day test:** None.

### 11. 'What-if' tool for sharing GPU computers between teams (quota and pre-emption rules) — NO-GO

- **Why:** NVIDIA and the free Kueue project are building the same simulator for free, and nobody publishes a price that anyone pays for this.
- **Biggest risk:** No buyer and no price, plus free tools from the platform owners.
- **Measured money:** None. No published price, and no paying-customer evidence was found.
- **Unit economics:** Unknown. At best a free add-on.
- **14-day test:** None.

## Recommendation

Build one free browser demo that tests the top two ideas together. Make it a five-puzzle coding game: you solve each level by writing a tiny program. Run it for 14 days. If it meets the pass bar (500 Steam wishlists, or 150 email sign-ups from 1,500 players, with a quarter of players finishing all five puzzles), build the full $9.99 game over 6-12 months. Plan for tens of thousands of dollars, not millions. If it fails, stop making games and go back to the drawing board. Drop every business and security tool on this list.

## What was killed and why

Nine of eleven are dead, for three reasons. (1) Someone already sells it at our price or gives it away: law citation checking (BriefCatch $60 a month), payment-page script checks (c/side free and Cloudflare free), flaky-test quarantine (Trunk free, Datadog), monitoring-bill trimming (Datadog built in, Grepr free), and GPU quota planning (NVIDIA and Kueue, free). (2) The money is not where the idea assumed: the big AI bills came from limits that switched off too late, not from people choosing the wrong limit. (3) The games are too crowded or too slow for one person: the screen-edge idle game (hundreds of copies, free leaders), the idle online game (one real earner, but 0 of 54 new solo games broke through, and it means years of live operations), and the Balatro-style and walking-RPG copies (0 of 35 solo luck roguelikes succeeded; the walking model needs two people and four years).

## What we could not check

- Whether the 'solo' comparables really are one person: this rests on Steam developer credits only. Replicube's maker also had an existing audience.
- Steam's release-date trap: Steam shows the full-release date, which hides years of early access. It inflated The Farmer Was Replaced, and I found it also applies to Orb of Creation (on Steam since 2022). Other comparables may carry it too.
- How many copies one Steam review stands for (30-50 is a rule of thumb, not measured), and the roughly 50% net share.
- The planned test of whether a free browser version beats a normal Steam demo (about 275 idle games from 2025) was never run.
- Whether the owner can reach programmers at all, which no one can know before the test.
- How much of Fantasy Hike's maker's revenue comes from the game.
- Whether BriefCatch's checker actually catches the bad citations courts identified.
- All pass and kill thresholds are our own pre-set guesses, not benchmarks.

## Process notes

- Every candidate that reached a red-team refuter in cycle 1 was killed; the finish run's finishers upheld every kill except the browser-first idle game, where a solo counter-example (Orb of Creation) was found.
- Steam's release date hides early access; it inflated two comparables (The Farmer Was Replaced, Orb of Creation). Treat review-count comparables as lifetime, not launch-year.
- The upgraded (two-pass, maximum effort) researcher archived 196 sources against the control's 54 and its numbers reproduced, but both chose the same top two (monitor difference score 0.7).
- Resuming a partially failed workflow re-ran many finished parallel agents; an economy finish from a condensed digest cost about 1.3M subagent tokens against about 17M for the two full runs.
- Owner channel: `apex-router mail` bus and e-mailed status; agents' questions were answered with standing rulings; the owner's own decisions were left to the owner.
